## Funding & Entity Strategy

Because we are a **NJ for-profit LLC selling into PA**, funding splits into three lanes. Every grant table in this plan tags its rows to one of these.

### The Three Funding Lanes

| Lane | How we get the money | Best-fit sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | We apply as the NJ LLC and receive funds ourselves | NJ state (home) + federal/national that fund for-profits (e.g., NSF SBIR) |
| **Partner-led** | A PA/NJ school, district, college, or nonprofit is the applicant; we are the funded tool | PAsmart, Dept of Ed, most school grants |
| **Sales** | Not a grant — we sell licenses to institutions | Erie & PA schools, districts, colleges, debate orgs, businesses |

**Correction to the original plan:** PA direct-to-company grants (`DCED`, `KIZ`, `Ben Franklin`) gate on a **qualifying PA business location** — and several education/foundation grants require **501(c)(3) status**. Our Erie apartment is a personal residence, not yet a registered/qualifying business location, so today those are *not* Direct sources — reach them Partner-led, via the nonprofit track, or by formalizing PA nexus (below).

### Nonprofit / Fiscal-Sponsor Track

Unlocks grants that only fund nonprofits/schools (`EITC/OSTC`, `Knight`, `Gates`, `Walton`, `Mozilla`, most PA/Erie foundations) — without changing the NJ LLC.

> **Reality check (important):** Fiscal sponsorship funds a **charitable program**, not the for-profit LLC's product or profit. In practice you run a program like a *"Debate Literacy Initiative"* (free access + coaching for underserved/Title I students, teacher PD, curriculum) under the sponsor; grant money pays for that mission delivery. The LLC can be paid a **fair-market, arm's-length** license/service fee by the program, but grants can't simply become company revenue. So treat this lane as funding **access & impact** (which also drives adoption), while **`NSF SBIR` + `CSIT` grants fund the actual product** (see those rows).

**Recommended:** start with **fiscal sponsorship** (fast, no new entity, ~10% fee). Skip forming your own 501(c)(3) unless the nonprofit-only pipeline clears ~$50K/yr — it adds a board, filings, and months of setup.

**Is "Side Project" a good option? Yes — worth a first call.** It's a legitimate 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor (est. 2012, EIN 46‑0769403) that is **explicitly focused on Western PA** — directly relevant to Erie — accepts *educational* projects, and charges the standard ~10%. Cons: it's **small** (~$147K annual revenue, ~15 projects), so it may lack capacity for complex federal grants, and its mission framing is "progressive social change / social justice" — position the initiative around **civic education, media literacy, and access equity** to fit. Best for **local Erie/PA foundation + EITC money** and running the charitable access program.

**Alternatives & trade-offs:**
- **Aspiration** — most *mission-aligned* for a civic-tech tool (tech/digital-rights focus, plus product/fundraising mentoring); leans open-source/non-commercial, so confirm comfort with a for-profit-linked project.
- **Community Partners** — best if you need a **big back-office that can administer government grants** (note the higher 15% govt fee); larger and less personal.
- **Tides** — most prestigious/scaled, but likely overkill and higher-touch than an early project needs.
- **Find more:** the [Fiscal Sponsor Directory](https://fiscalsponsordirectory.org/) lets you filter by mission and geography.

### PA-Nexus: What the Erie Apartment Changes

The Erie apartment means we likely **already have a PA footprint** — which cuts both ways:

- [ ] **We may already be "doing business" in PA.** Operating from Erie can, by itself, trigger a duty to register as a foreign LLC and to handle PA taxes — this may not be optional. File the **[Foreign Registration Statement (DSCB:15-412)](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dos/programs/business/types-of-filings-and-registrations/foreign-associations)** (~$250) + docketing statement. ⚠️ **Mallory caveat:** registering subjects the LLC to PA courts' *general* personal jurisdiction (suable in PA for activities arising anywhere) — a real trade-off to weigh with an attorney.
- [ ] **PA SaaS sales tax** — PA taxes SaaS; once we sell to PA customers we must register with PA Revenue and collect. This applies regardless of the nexus decision.
- [ ] **KIZ tax credits (conditional).** Up to **$100K/yr in sellable credits**, but only if we (1) sit *inside* a [Keystone Innovation Zone](https://dced.pa.gov/programs/keystone-innovation-zone-tax-credit-program/) boundary (a residential apartment likely isn't — a co-working desk in Erie's KIZ near Gannon / Penn State Behrend could be), (2) are <8 yrs old, (3) fit a target sector (ed-tech's fit under "Robotics and Technology" is **unconfirmed** — ask the local KIZ Coordinator), and (4) have PA revenue growth. Apply via grants.pa.gov by **Dec 1** yearly.

**Bottom line:** Because we already have an Erie address, *formalizing* PA nexus is cheap (~$250 + tax compliance) — so the real decision is the **Mallory jurisdiction exposure**, not cost. Foreign registration also unlocks **[DCED programs](https://dced.pa.gov/programs/)** and cleaner PA public-sector contracting.

> **Verify with a CPA/attorney:** whether the Erie apartment already triggers PA foreign-registration + tax obligations, PA SaaS sales-tax setup, KIZ zone boundaries/sector fit, and the *Mallory* general-jurisdiction trade-off.

## What's Missing or Weak
1. Customer discovery before pricing — The price tiers are guesses right now. Before locking them in, you need 5–10 conversations with debate coaches to understand budget authority, purchasing processes, and willingness to pay. Add a validation step before Phase 2 pricing.

2. Grant timelines are vague — "Not applied" doesn't help you act. ECGRA Ignite Erie has specific application windows (usually spring/fall). Erie Community Foundation has quarterly deadlines. The plan should map to actual grant cycles so you don't miss them.

3. Distribution mechanics — How do students actually get the app? School IT departments are gatekeepers. You need to address: PWA install flow, Chromebook compatibility (most Erie schools are 1:1 Chromebooks), whether IT needs to whitelist the domain, COPPA/FERPA compliance documentation ready before approaching schools.

4. Erie-specific funding you're missing:

Jefferson Educational Society — actively funds civic education projects in Erie
Erie Innovation District / Flagship Opportunity Zone — tech startup incentives
Edinboro University / Penn State Behrend — academic partnerships, student interns, institutional pilot sites
United Way of Erie County — education initiative funding
Erie Regional Chamber — startup spotlight / pitch competitions
5. No competitive positioning — A grant reviewer or school admin will ask "why not just use Google Docs + a rubric?" You need a one-paragraph answer in this plan.

6. Sustainability gap — What happens when Year 1 grant money is spent? The plan jumps from "free pilots" to "$800/school" without explaining how you convert free users to paying customers when the grant expires.

7. Who does the work? — Is this just you? Approaching 6+ schools, writing 4+ grant applications, and building features simultaneously is a lot for one person. Consider noting whether you need a co-founder, part-time biz dev help, or a Gannon SBDC advisor.

8. Impact measurement for grants — Grant applications need measurable outcomes. Add specific KPIs: "Students using Debate Dissector will identify 30% more logical fallacies in practice debates" or similar.
